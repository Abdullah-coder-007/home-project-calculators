    How the tile calculator works

    The calculator adds up your area, adds a waste allowance for cuts and breakage, and divides by the space one tile covers including its grout joint.

    Area to buy = area × (1 + waste %)
    Space per tile (sq ft) = (tile length + joint) × (tile width + joint) ÷ 144
    Tiles = area to buy ÷ space per tile, rounded up
    Boxes = area to buy ÷ square feet per box, rounded up

    Worked example

    A 10 by 10 foot bathroom floor is 100 sq ft. With a straight layout, 10% extra makes 110 sq ft. A 12 by 12 inch tile with a 1/8 inch joint covers 1.02 sq ft, so you need 108 tiles, or 8 boxes of 15 sq ft.

    Waste allowance by layout

    LayoutTypical extraWhy
    Straight (grid or brick offset)10%Cuts only along the edges
    Diagonal15%Every edge tile is cut at an angle
    Herringbone or chevron20%Many angled cuts and offcuts you cannot reuse
    Large tiles (24 in and up)Add 5%Each broken tile is a bigger loss

    Measuring an area to tile

    1. Measure wall to wall. For floors, tile usually runs under the toilet but not under the vanity cabinet or built-in tub.
    2. Split L-shaped rooms into rectangles with Add a section.
    3. Use a cut-out section for areas you will not tile, such as a kitchen island or a vanity.
    4. For a backsplash, measure the width of each wall run and the height from countertop to the bottom of the upper cabinets, then subtract windows and outlets larger than a tile.

    Grout and thinset estimates

    Grout fills the joints between tiles, so the amount depends on the joint volume. The calculator works out the joint volume per square foot and converts it to pounds, then adds 10%. Small mosaic tiles with wide joints can need several times more grout than large tiles.

    Grout (cubic inches per sq ft) = 144 × (tile length + tile width) × joint × thickness ÷ (tile length × tile width)

    Thinset coverage depends on the trowel. The calculator uses approximate coverage per 50 lb bag of 95 sq ft for a 1/4 by 1/4 inch notch, 70 sq ft for 1/4 by 3/8 inch and 45 sq ft for 1/2 by 1/2 inch. Back-buttering large tiles uses more. Check the chart on your mortar bag before buying.

    Before you buy

    • Check that every box shows the same lot or batch number; shades vary between lots.
    • Lay out a few rows dry before setting any tile to see where cuts will fall.
    • Unused full boxes can often be returned. Keep the receipt and do not open boxes until you need them.

    Common questions

    How much extra tile should I buy?

    Add about 10% for a straight layout, 15% for diagonal, and 15 to 20% for herringbone or large tiles that break easily. Rooms with lots of corners and cuts need the higher end.

    How many 12x12 tiles do I need for 100 square feet?

    Each 12 by 12 inch tile covers 1 sq ft, slightly more with a grout joint. For 100 sq ft with 10% extra you need about 108 tiles at a 1/8 inch joint.

    How much grout do I need?

    It depends on tile size, joint width and tile thickness. Small tiles with wide joints use far more grout than large tiles with thin joints. The calculator estimates the pounds and you should confirm with the coverage chart on the grout bag.

    Which trowel size should I use?

    As a rough guide: 1/4 by 1/4 inch square notch for tiles up to about 8 inches, 1/4 by 3/8 inch for 8 to 16 inch tiles, and 1/2 by 1/2 inch for larger tiles. Always follow the tile and mortar maker's instructions.

    Should I keep spare tiles?

    Yes. Keep at least a few tiles, ideally a full box, from the same lot. Matching a discontinued tile years later for a repair can be impossible.
