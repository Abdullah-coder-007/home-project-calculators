Green got the headlines this fall, but brown has been quietly collecting Colors of the Year. Dunn-Edwards picked **French Press Coffee**, C2 Paint picked **Pueblo** and Little Greene picked **Marle**, all for 2027. This week Forbes ran a story on why designers are "ditching white walls for rich brown," and the numbers back it up: according to the paint-sampling company Samplize, quoted in that story, brown now appears on 13.4% of homeowners' paint shortlists, up from 9.2% two years ago.

If you're thinking about a brown dining room or a cocoa powder room this winter, here's how the new browns differ and how much paint and primer to buy.

## The 2027 browns

| Brand | Color | What it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dunn-Edwards | French Press Coffee (DEBN07) | A deep espresso brown with hints of plum and charcoal |
| C2 Paint | Pueblo (C2-510) | A warm, earthy brown with rose undertones |
| Little Greene | Marle | A soft, clay-inspired mid-tone brown |

They're not interchangeable. French Press Coffee is very dark: Dunn-Edwards lists its light reflectance value (LRV) as **6**, on a scale where 0 is black and 100 is white. That's darker than Benjamin Moore's Webster Green, at about 20. Dunn-Edwards' color marketing manager, Lauren Hoferkamp, puts it this way: "It's rich enough to make a statement but still behaves like a neutral."

Pueblo changes with the light. C2 says it shifts through the day, and Forbes describes it as brighter and more plum-toned in daylight and browner in the evening. Marle is the gentlest of the three. Little Greene's creative director, Ruth Mottershead, describes it as heavy on yellow ochre, with plenty of black to soften it.

## Where the browns work

The brands' own suggestions lean toward rooms you use in the evening, where a dark color feels cozy rather than gloomy:

- **Dining rooms, powder rooms and entryways.** HGTV lists all three as places for French Press Coffee, and suggests mirrors and metallic finishes to bounce light around.
- **Bedrooms, bathrooms and living rooms.** Little Greene pitches Marle for any room meant to feel restful.
- **Front doors and exteriors.** HGTV suggests French Press Coffee as an alternative to black on dramatic exteriors.

For pairings, Forbes' designers suggest checking the undertone first. Browns with red or yellow undertones are warm and sit well with cream and tan. Browns with gray or taupe undertones are cool and go with soft blues, sage and evergreen. One Chicago designer's favorite pairing is chocolate brown with powder blue.

## How much paint you need

These examples use the same math as our paint calculator: two coats at 350 square feet per gallon, a door counted as 3 × 7 ft (21 sq ft) and a window as 3 × 5 ft (15 sq ft). Trim isn't included. Check the coverage on your can, since it varies by product.

**1. A brown dining room, 12 × 12 ft with an 8 ft ceiling, one door and two windows**

- Walls: 2 × (12 + 12) × 8 = 384 sq ft
- Minus the door and windows (51 sq ft): 333 sq ft
- Two coats: 666 sq ft ÷ 350 = 1.90 gallons
- **Buy 2 gallons** of the brown.
- Primer, one coat: 333 ÷ 350 = 0.95 gallons, so **1 gallon**.

**2. A drenched powder room, 5 × 6 ft with an 8 ft ceiling and one door**

"Drenching" means walls and ceiling in the same color.

- Walls: 2 × (5 + 6) × 8 = 176 sq ft, minus the door: 155 sq ft
- Ceiling: 5 × 6 = 30 sq ft
- Two coats: walls 0.89 gallons, ceiling 0.17 gallons
- **Buy 1 gallon plus 1 quart.**
- Primer: 185 sq ft ÷ 350 = 0.53 gallons. That's more than two quarts, so the calculator suggests **1 gallon**.

If you skip the primer and the brown needs a third coat to look even, the powder room walls alone go to 1.33 gallons, so you'd be buying 2 gallons for the walls instead of 1.

Buying the right amount matters a little more this year. On its October 27 earnings call, Sherwin-Williams said the 8% price increase in its paint stores, which took effect September 1, is "tracking as planned."

> **Fun fact:** Dark doesn't always mean more coats. Sherwin-Williams' color experts say muted, grayed-off colors made with colorants like iron oxides or black hide better than bright, clean yellows, oranges and reds, which use more transparent pigments. So a soft, desaturated brown like Marle will usually cover more easily than a fire-engine red. Set your coats and primer in the [paint calculator](/paint-calculator/) to see what your room needs.

## Tips before you buy

1. **Use a gray-tinted primer.** Sherwin-Williams says a primer tinted to the right shade of gray helps deep colors reach their true color in fewer coats. Its chips carry a code from P1 (lightest) to P6 (darkest) on the back, so ask the store to tint to that.
2. **Test it in your light.** French Press Coffee's plum undertone and Pueblo's rose undertone change between day and evening. Paint a large swatch on two walls and look at it at night, when you'll use a dining room most.
3. **Buy all your gallons at once.** Deep tints can vary slightly between batches. Buy everything together, or mix the cans in one bucket before you start.
4. **Measure the odd bits.** A powder room with a sloped ceiling or a dining room with a bay window is easy to underestimate.
