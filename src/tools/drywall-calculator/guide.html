How the drywall calculator works

The calculator finds the wall area, takes off doors and windows, adds the ceiling if you choose, and divides by the area of one sheet with an allowance for cuts.

Wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height − 21 sq ft per door − 15 sq ft per window
Sheets = (wall area + ceiling) × (1 + waste) ÷ sheet area

Worked example

A 14 by 12 foot room with 8 foot walls has 416 sq ft of wall. One door and two windows take off 51 sq ft, leaving 365 sq ft. The 168 sq ft ceiling brings it to 533 sq ft. With 10% extra that is 586 sq ft, or 19 sheets of 4 by 8 drywall.

Finishing materials

MaterialRule of thumb used
ScrewsAbout 1 per sq ft of board (32 per 4 by 8 sheet)
Ready-mixed joint compoundAbout 9 gallons per 1,000 sq ft (tape coat plus two finish coats)
Paper tapeAbout 380 ft per 1,000 sq ft, in 250 ft rolls
Corner beadOne length per outside corner per 8 ft of wall height

Compound use varies a lot with skill and finish level, so treat it as a starting point. It is easy to buy another bucket.

Before you buy

  • Hang the ceiling first, then the walls, so wall sheets support the ceiling edges.
  • Hang wall sheets horizontally on 8 foot walls; it usually means fewer seams.
  • Check that your vehicle can carry the sheet length, or ask about delivery.

Sources
