How the paint calculator works

Paint is bought by the gallon, but it is used by the square foot. So the calculator finds the wall area you will actually paint, multiplies it by the number of coats, and divides by how far one gallon spreads.

Wall area = 2 × (length + width) × height − doors − windows
Gallons = wall area × coats ÷ coverage per gallon

Each door counts as 21 sq ft (3 by 7 feet) and each window as 15 sq ft (3 by 5 feet). If your doors or windows are much bigger, such as a patio slider, add an extra door or two to the count.

Worked example

A 14 by 12 foot bedroom with 8 foot walls has a perimeter of 52 feet, so 416 sq ft of wall. One door and two windows remove 51 sq ft, leaving 365 sq ft. Two coats at 350 sq ft per gallon need 2.09 gallons, which means 2 gallons plus 1 quart. If the leftover is more than a quarter gallon, the calculator rounds up to another full gallon, because two or more quarts usually cost more than a gallon.

Measuring your room

  1. Measure the length and width of the floor along the walls, ignoring baseboards.
  2. Measure wall height from floor to ceiling. Standard ceilings are 8 feet; many newer homes have 9 feet.
  3. Count doors and windows on the walls you are painting. Closets with doors count as a door.
  4. For a room with angled or uneven walls, add up the widths of all the walls, divide by two, and enter that as the length with a width of 0. The calculator then uses your exact total wall length.

Before you buy

  • Buy all the paint for one color at the same time and ask the store to mix it together ("box" it) so every can matches.
  • Keep a little leftover in a sealed container, labeled with the color name and room, for touch-ups.
  • Ceilings are usually painted in flat white ceiling paint, so the calculator lists it separately from wall paint.
  • Trim and doors need a separate semi-gloss paint; one quart covers the trim in most rooms.

Sources

The calculator uses 350 sq ft per gallon per coat, a little below most brands' published rates, so you are less likely to run short on textured or porous walls.
