How the insulation calculator works

Batts are sold by the bag, and each bag label says how many square feet it covers. Blown-in insulation is sold by the bag too, but how far a bag goes depends on how deep you blow it, so every bag carries a coverage chart: bags per 1,000 sq ft for each R-value. The calculator finds your area, then uses the label or the chart.

Wall area = wall length × height − 21 sq ft per door − 15 sq ft per window
Batt bags = area ÷ square feet per bag, rounded up
Existing R = existing depth (in) × R per inch
R to add = target R − existing R
Blown-in bags = attic area × bags per 1,000 sq ft for that R ÷ 1,000, rounded up

When the R-value you need falls between two rows of the chart, the calculator reads between them in a straight line. It does the same for the depth to blow.

Worked example: walls

A house has 120 ft of outside wall, 8 ft high, with 2 doors and 10 windows. That is 960 sq ft, minus 42 sq ft of doors and 150 sq ft of windows, so 768 sq ft. R-13 batts for a 2x4 wall come in bags of 106.56 sq ft, so you need 7.2 bags: 8 bags.

Worked example: topping up an attic

A 1,000 sq ft attic has 3 1/2 in of old loose fiberglass, worth about R-8.75 (3.5 × 2.5). To reach R-49 you need to add about R-40. On the fiberglass chart that sits between R-38 (19.5 bags, 13 in) and R-44 (23 bags, 14 3/4 in), so you need 20.8 bags per 1,000 sq ft: 21 bags, blown about 13 3/4 in deep on top of what is there. With cellulose it is 57 bags, about 11 in deep.

Measuring

  1. Walls: measure the length of each outside wall (or each wall you are insulating) along the floor and add them up. Measure height from floor to ceiling.
  2. Attic: measure the attic floor, or use the outside size of the house minus the overhang. Split L-shaped houses into rectangles with Add a section.
  3. Existing insulation: push a ruler down to the ceiling drywall in a few spots away from the eaves and use a typical depth.

Before you buy

  • Seal air leaks first: gaps around light fixtures, pipes, chimneys and the attic hatch. Insulation does not stop air moving through those holes.
  • Fit baffles at the eaves so blown insulation does not block the soffit vents, and keep insulation the required distance from recessed lights that are not rated IC and from chimneys.
  • Do not disturb vermiculite (gray-brown, pebbly loose fill) until it has been tested for asbestos.
  • Wear a respirator, goggles, long sleeves and gloves. Fiberglass and cellulose dust both irritate.
